Apply the five-whys decision methodology to the question below.

Don't answer the question as posed — REFRAME first, then answer.

1. **Recognize the trigger** — confirm this is genuinely low/mid confidence. If you'd answer with high confidence, just decide.
2. **Why 1** — Why is this a decision at all? What's the underlying need? (Not "which option?" but "what am I trying to accomplish?")
3. **Why 2** — Why does the system constrain us this way? What's forcing the fork?
4. **Why 3** — Why is this functionality actually required? Is it real or inherited assumption?
5. **Why 4** — Is this decision appropriate for this platform/scope? Does it belong HERE?
6. **Why 5 (most important — don't skip)** — Why are we stuck? What earlier choice put us at this fork? Is there an upstream fix that would DISSOLVE this decision rather than answer it? **May iterate up to 2 more times if chasing the upstream chain reveals another bad choice further up. Hard cap at 7 total whys.**
7. **Stop the cascade** when any of: clear reframe found / a why returns "I don't know" (surface as escalation point, don't fabricate) / two consecutive whys return the same answer / 7-why cap reached.
8. **Apply /best-practices to the REFRAMED question** — not the original. Most reframed questions become high-confidence BP-answerable at this step.
9. **Decide or escalate honestly:**
   - Dissolved → take the dissolved-question path
   - BP answers it → apply BP
   - Genuine trade-off → escalate with structured framing (reframing context + clean trade-off + recommendation if any), NOT "what do you want?"

The failure mode to avoid: accepting the framing as given. Low/mid confidence is a signal that the QUESTION is wrong, not that the decision is hard. Most hard decisions become easy once reframed.
